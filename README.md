A heist game about changing the past. Linden Square is a paper architectural model you can
turn in your hands. Visit it in **1946**, **1986** and **2026**. Plant a tree, reroute a
drain, save an alley, then watch the consequences ripple into the present. Finally, pull off the
job in 2026 through the openings history left behind.

Built with Three.js and TypeScript (Vite). Every visible 3D object comes from Blender:
the scenes are generated by Blender Python scripts, saved as editable `.blend` files and exported
to glTF 2.0. The game needs no server, API key or external service.

## Run it

Requirements:

- Node.js 20 or newer (tested with 22.22).
- A desktop browser with **WebGL 2**: current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev            # http://127.0.0.1:5173
```

Production build:

```bash
npm run build          # type-checks, then bundles into dist/
npm run preview        # serves dist/ at http://127.0.0.1:4173
```

`dist/` is fully static and uses relative paths, so any static host or sub-folder works.

Useful URL options:

- `?nointro` skips the opening sequence.
- `#plan=1.c1.Oy-Pk` loads a shared plan (see [Plan links](#plan-links)).

### Controls

| | Planning | The job (heist) |
|---|---|---|
| Left-drag | rotate the model | rotate the model |
| Right-drag / Shift+drag | pan | pan |
| Mouse wheel | zoom | zoom |
| Click | inspect an object (opens its card) | walk there, or use the file/diamond/box, the junction box or the getaway bicycle |
| Hover | highlight what can be inspected | preview the path (green = reachable, amber rings = waits at a door, red = blocked) |
| `1` `2` `3` | switch to 1946 / 1986 / 2026 | — |
| `Ctrl/Cmd+Z` | undo the last change | — |
| `H` | hints | — |
| `R` | reset the camera | — |
| `Space` (hold) | — | wait in place |
| `Esc` | close the card or dialog | pause (resume, restart, settings, back to planning) |

Click, or press `Esc`, to skip the intro or the replay. The settings dialog holds:

- volume and mute;
- reduced motion (era changes become instant cuts, camera moves jump);
- visual quality: low has no shadows or post-processing; high uses a sharper shadow map, MSAA and full pixel ratio;
- an FPS / draw-call counter.

## How to play

### Eras and the plan

The same neighborhood exists in three eras, colour-coded **ochre 1946**, **teal 1986** and
**coral 2026**. Colour is never the only cue: every era button, entry and card also carries its
year.

Click an object (the oak sapling, the storm-drain survey, Brandt's workshop, the alley notice, …)
to open its card. The card shows:

- what the object is in the current era;
- what can be changed there, with the cost of each option;
- the consequences you have already discovered.

The plan has nine interventions across 1946 and 1986. Each deviation from the original history
costs 1 from the contract budget. The **Changes** counter shows `used / budget`. Undo and reset
refund changes.

Some changes depend on others. For example, a maintenance hatch needs the creek drain, and the
alley service door needs the alley kept. When a prerequisite stops holding, the dependent change
is removed, a message explains why, and Ctrl+Z brings both back.

History is **recomputed from scratch on every change** by a deterministic rule list
(`src/sim/causality.ts`). Nothing depends on the order you clicked in, and switching eras never
accumulates effects.

### Consequences

Switching era replays the change on the model. Façades fold up and floors rise. Canopies unfurl.
Removed objects flatten into the base while a tracing-paper ghost of the old version fades.
Changed places pulse amber, and amber arcs link each cause to its effect. Plan changes are held
until a transition settles.

Some outcomes help, some hurt, several surprise:

- a preserved yard oak lets you climb onto the annex roof, and it also hides part of the yard from camera C3;
- the bank later installs roof camera C6, and the manager's car moves to the lane;
- keeping the alley open sends guard B on a new round through it, and moves the delivery van to Linden Lane;
- the creek drain opens a tunnel under the bank, and it floods Café Kopp in 2003 unless a 1986 valve is fitted;
- the square oak shades the front service door from camera C5, but its protected roots take the café terrace;
- the yard oak plus the kept alley pushes the bank's 1987 expansion onto the unprotected garden,
  and the construction crane fells the oak. The garden petition fixes it.

Planning aids:

- **Compare with original** shows the untouched history as a translucent silhouette and tints what changed.
- **Patrols & vision** draws guard routes with pause times, plus live vision cones in 2026.
- **Underground** turns everything above ground into tracing paper so drains and the basement are visible.

### The job

Press **Start the job**. The plan is frozen and 2026 plays in real time. Reach the target,
then escape to the getaway bicycle at the west end of Riverdale Street.

Stealth rules:

- **Guards** see 90° up to 6 m on their own floor. They also notice anyone within about a
  metre, whichever way they face.
- **Cameras** sweep or rotate. The live-monitored lamppost camera C5 reacts 2.5× faster.
- **Line of sight** is blocked by walls, closed doors, canopies, the parked van and other
  objects. Nobody sees through walls or between floors.
- **Detection** is gradual. The meter fills faster the closer the observer is and drains when
  you are unseen. Each new sighting counts as a detection event; a full meter means you are caught.
- **The service door** opens only while the delivery courier props it open. A fixed 30-second
  cycle is shown in the heist panel.
- **The alarm circuit** can be cut at Brandt's junction box, which only exists if the 1946
  wiring went through his workshop. In contract 2 the circuit powers the Glass Room laser and
  dome camera; in contract 3 it powers the vault maglock and the basement camera.

When you are caught, the game names who saw you, when and where, and offers **Retry with the
same plan** or **Back to planning**.

### Contracts

| | Contract | Target | Budget | The problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The First Withdrawal** | the Linden file (basement archive) | 5 | The bank is sealed at street level. Get in at all. |
| 2 | **The Glass Diamond** | the diamond (upper-floor Glass Room) | 5 | Access, plus a way past the laser curtain and the rotating dome camera. |
| 3 | **A Clean Timeline** | deposit box 46 (vault, maglock) | 3 | Cut the alarm circuit **and** keep the garden and Café Kopp. |

Each contract unlocks the next. Contract 1 has three route families, each through real geometry,
plus a combined one:

1. **Rooftop:** oak planted by the service yard (1946) and preserved (1986) → climb to the annex roof → upper service door.
2. **Underground:** drain along the creek (1946) and a maintenance hatch (1986) → manhole → sewer → boiler room.
3. **Service:** alley kept and the service door moved to the alley (1986) → slip in while the courier props the door.
4. **Combined:** oak in the square (1946), preserved (1986) → its canopy hides the front service door from C5 during the square delivery.

Progressive hints (`H`) go from a direction, to specifics, to the answer for one obstacle.

### After the job

A successful job plays a **replay**:

1. the sealed original 2026;
2. each of your changes in its own era;
3. what they did to the present;
4. the route you took;
5. the theft and the escape, from the recorded run.

The **results** screen lists:

- the contract;
- the changes and budget used;
- the time;
- detection events;
- the extra conditions;
- the approach.

From there you can go to the next contract, try another solution, replay, reset the timeline, or
copy a link to the plan. Practice runs, which start when a contract's conditions are not met,
never unlock anything.

### Plan links

**Copy plan link** writes the plan into the URL, for example `#plan=1.c1.Oy-Pk`, and copies it.
Opening that link loads the plan. Every token is validated: format version, contract, intervention
and option codes, duplicates, budget and length. A malformed or tampered link is rejected with a
message and your own plan is kept. A link to a contract you have not unlocked is refused.

### Saving

Progress is saved in `localStorage`: unlocked and completed contracts, the plan per contract,
discovered consequences, hint levels and settings. A corrupted save is sanitized field by field
instead of crashing. If storage is unavailable the game still runs; progress then lasts for the
session only.

## Project layout

```
src/
  sim/        pure TypeScript, no Three.js: causality, plan rules, layout expansion,
              A* navigation, line of sight, the fixed-step heist simulation, scripted runs
  data/       layout.json (the single spatial source), variants.json, interventions,
              contracts, security, decor, text.en.ts (all English copy)
  game/       save/load, plan links, contract progression
  render/     asset loading and caching, paper materials, stage and post-processing, camera rig,
              world variants, era transitions, cutaway/X-ray, overlays, characters
  audio/      procedural Web Audio engine (no samples)
  ui/         DOM case-file interface and CSS
  app/        App (the game orchestrator), Replay, planning sites
blender/
  scripts/    Blender Python that builds every asset (build_all.py runs them all)
  source/     generated, editable .blend files (one per asset)
  fonts/      Liberation Serif (SIL OFL) for cut-out lettering
public/
  assets/models/*.glb   glTF 2.0 exports      public/assets/manifest.json   asset manifest
  draco/                Draco decoder (from three.js)
tests/        Vitest unit tests        e2e/   Playwright end-to-end suite
docs/         DESIGN.md (rules and content), ASSETS.md (Blender pipeline)
```

`src/data/layout.json` drives navigation, line of sight **and** the Blender wall, door and portal
generation, so what you see and what the rules use cannot drift apart. Tests check that every
door and portal anchor in the exported models matches the layout.

A development asset viewer is served at `http://127.0.0.1:5173/viewer.html` while `npm run dev` runs.

## Blender assets

Eight assets (terrain, bank, café, workshop, townhouses, trees, props, characters) are generated
by `blender/scripts/*.py` with **Blender 5.0.1**, run headless through the `bpy` Python module.

- The `.blend` sources are in `blender/source/`.
- The exports are in `public/assets/models/` (glTF binary, Draco-compressed geometry, WEBP textures).
- `public/assets/manifest.json` maps stable ids to models, variants, anchors and animation clips.

Totals: 113,341 triangles and 2,020 KB of `.glb` for the whole neighborhood in all three eras.
Two consecutive builds produce byte-identical `.glb` files.

```bash
python3.11 -m venv ~/blender-venv && ~/blender-venv/bin/pip install bpy==5.0.1
~/blender-venv/bin/python blender/scripts/build_all.py            # all assets, about 5 s
~/blender-venv/bin/python blender/scripts/build_all.py -- bank    # one asset
~/blender-venv/bin/python blender/scripts/verify_blend.py         # opens every .blend
```

`npm run assets` and `npm run assets:verify` run the same two scripts using `$BLENDER_PYTHON`,
falling back to `python3`.

See **[docs/ASSETS.md](docs/ASSETS.md)** for the conventions (naming, custom properties, variant
registry, pivots, level/side groups for the cutaway, anchors, proxies, animation clips) and the
manifest format.

## Testing and verification

```bash
npm run typecheck
npm test                           # Vitest unit tests (about 1 minute)
npm run build && npm run preview   # then, in another terminal:
npm run e2e                        # Playwright suite against http://127.0.0.1:4173/
node e2e/run-all.mjs http://127.0.0.1:5173/   # the same suite against the dev server
```

The end-to-end suite drives the real game in headless Chromium. It clicks the canvas, the DOM
and the keyboard, and fails on any console error or warning, uncaught exception, failed request
or HTTP error. It writes `e2e/out/report.json` and screenshots.

### Results (run on 2026-09-28 in this repository's container)

- **Type check:** clean.
- **Unit tests:** 58 passed. They cover:
  - causality: determinism, invalidation, no cycles, budget and refunds;
  - plan links and saves against garbage input;
  - layout and geometry agreement;
  - navigation and line of sight: no sight through walls, floors or canopies; guard hearing;
  - every reference solution solved and replayed offline, and the untouched timeline failing;
  - the planning preview animating the present without recording a replay;
  - the manifest agreeing with the variant registry.
- **Blender:** all 8 `.blend` files open in Blender 5.0.1 (`verify_blend.py`), and a full rebuild reproduces the `.glb` files byte for byte.
- **Production build:** clean.
- **End-to-end: 12/12 passed on the production build, and 12/12 on the dev server (`npm run dev`).**
  The nine live solutions finished at identical times on both.

### Performance, measured honestly

The only renderer available here is **headless Chromium with SwiftShader**, which renders WebGL
on the CPU. At 1280×720 on this container, across three suite runs, it measured:

| quality | frames per second | draw calls | triangles drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| low | 7.9–8.3 | 332–335 | 51,600–53,500 |
| medium | 4.2–4.3 | 650 (includes the shadow pass) | about 102,000 |

Counts vary a little with which characters are on screen at the moment of sampling.

These numbers describe a software rasterizer. They say nothing about frame rates on a GPU, and
**no GPU frame rate has been measured**. The simulation runs at a fixed 30 Hz regardless of frame
rate, so slow rendering never changes the outcome of a heist. The in-game FPS counter
(Settings) reports measured values on your own machine.

First load of the production build is about 3.4 MB uncompressed:

- models: 2.0 MB;
- JavaScript: 0.8 MB (221 KB gzipped);
- Draco decoder: 0.25 MB;
- manifest: 0.19 MB (17 KB gzipped);
- fonts: 0.15 MB (only the Latin subsets used);
- CSS: 32 KB.

On the local preview server (headless, same machine) the planning screen was up about 0.8 s after navigation.

## Limitations and known issues

- **No GPU verification.**
  - All rendering checks ran on software WebGL (SwiftShader). Visual quality was judged from those screenshots.
  - Frame rate on real graphics hardware is unmeasured.
  - The "high" preset (MSAA, 2048 shadow map) was exercised for errors but not visually compared on a GPU.
- **Browsers:** only Chromium (headless) was tested. Firefox and Safari are expected to work, since the game needs WebGL 2 and standard DOM APIs, but they were not run.
- **Audio** is procedural. Tests only confirm that it starts after a user gesture and runs without errors; nobody listened to it during development.
- **Touch:** the game is designed for a mouse. Tap and one-finger drag work through pointer events, but there is no pinch-zoom and phones show a notice. The layout does fit a 390 px wide screen.
- **Timing:** in-game transitions and replays are timed in real seconds with a 0.1 s frame cap. On very slow machines they play in slow motion rather than skipping frames.
- **The replay** plays the recorded run from 10 Hz snapshots with interpolation. It is faithful in position and timing, not frame-exact.
- **Guard hearing** is a fixed radius. Guards do not investigate noises or search after losing sight; they resume their rounds.
- **Draw calls:**
  - About 330 at low quality, well within desktop budgets, but static scenery is not merged.
  - The Vite build also copies three.js's own Draco decoder files into `dist/assets/`. The game loads the vendored copy in `public/draco/`, so those files (about 1.3 MB) are never downloaded.
- **The `window.__bj` test hook** is present in production builds. It exists for the e2e suite and debugging, and allows cheating from the console.
